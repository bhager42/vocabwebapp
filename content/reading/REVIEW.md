# Reading story reviews

Every story gets a review before it ships (PLAN.md Phase 12). `npm run reading:check` covers the
mechanical rules; this file records the checks a script cannot make:

1. **Holds together:** names, places, the order of events and facts agree throughout, and there
   is a clear beginning, middle and end.
2. **Answers are right:** each literal answer is in the story, and each inference answer is the
   one best answer the story supports, without being written out in it.
3. **No second right answer:** each wrong answer was checked against the story on its own.
4. **Needs the story:** no question can be answered from general knowledge alone.
5. **Suits the grade:** words, sentence length and themes; nothing frightening or mature.
6. **Original:** not a retelling of a known book, film or game.

Each entry lists anything that was changed. The parent's review comes after this one.

## Grade 3

The first five stories were the pilot; the other ten complete the grade. Fifteen in all:

### the-roof-garden: The Roof Garden

- **Holds together:** yes. The pots go up to the roof, and Grandpa is downstairs when Priya
  cheers and when she runs down to tell him. Three of the six pots are emptied and replanted.
  Mrs. Lee lives on the same floor, so she is one of the neighbors given tomatoes.
- **Answers:** Q2 (why she cheered) follows straight after the sprouts appear. Q3 (what happened
  to the sprouts) is never stated; torn leaves, the pigeon sitting there and Grandpa's net all
  point to the pigeon.
- **Wrong answers:** Q2's "school was over for the year" does not fit: it is spring and she
  still goes to school. Q3's "wind" would not pull sprouts out and leave torn leaves, and nothing
  suggests Priya forgot to water them, since she checked every day.
- **Needs the story:** yes; seeds, the pigeon and the net are all specific to it.
- **Grade:** plain words, family setting, a small problem solved.
- **Changed:** nothing.

### the-missing-library-book: The Missing Library Book

- **Holds together:** fixed one slip. Leo was outside on the porch, then "just then" heard a
  voice from his sister's room. He now goes back inside first.
- **Answers:** Q3 (why he worried): he has looked everywhere and wonders what to tell the
  librarian. Q4 (why Nina's face fell): it falls the moment he asks for the book back and lifts
  when he offers her a library trip.
- **Wrong answers:** Q3's "had not finished reading it" is ruled out because he read it three
  times. Q4's "scared of the sharks" is ruled out because she is reassuring her teddy bear.
- **Needs the story:** yes.
- **Grade:** read at 1.3 at first, below the grade 3 range. Some short sentences were joined
  and a few richer words added ("photographs", "cushions"); it now reads at 2.0, the bottom of
  the range. Worth the parent's view on whether it is still too easy.
- **Changed:** the porch slip above. The sequence question was rewritten too ("Which of these
  did Leo do first?"), because the old one asked what happened "right after" the porch, and
  that answer changed with the fix.

### busy-builders: Busy Builders (nonfiction)

- **Holds together:** yes; body, then dams, then lodges, then how beavers help other animals.
- **Facts checked:** beavers live across much of North America; their front teeth never stop
  growing; their back feet are webbed; dams are built from sticks, mud and stones; lodge
  entrances are under water; drained beaver ponds leave rich soil where meadows grow.
  "In the middle of the pond" was too absolute, since lodges are also built at the edge of a
  pond, so it now says "Out in the pond, beavers often build…".
- **Answers:** Q4 (why the doors are under water) is close to stated ("most other animals
  cannot"), but the reason, safety, still has to be worked out. Q5 (if the dam broke) needs the
  child to see that the pond exists only because the dam holds the water back.
- **Wrong answers:** none fit the text. Q4's "beavers are not able to climb" is not in the
  passage and does not explain an underwater door.
- **Needs the story:** mostly. A child who already knows about beavers could answer Q1 and Q4
  without reading. Nonfiction always carries some of that; flagging it for the parent.
- **Grade:** "lodge" is taught in the passage and asked as the vocabulary question.
- **Changed:** "In the middle of the pond", above.

### the-fun-run: The Fun Run

- **Holds together:** yes; two laps, she leads after the first and falls in the second.
- **Answers:** Q3 (why Ava stopped): she asks if Rosa is okay and holds out her hand. Q4 (why

- **Wrong answers:** Q1's "slipped in a puddle" does not fit a bright, warm day. Q3's "hurt her
  own knee" does not fit, since it is Rosa's knee that stings.
- **Needs the story:** Q5 (the lesson) could be guessed by a child who has heard many stories
  like this. That is acceptable for a main-idea question, whose wrong answers are clearly off.
- **Grade:** a fall and tears, but nothing more upsetting; the ending is warm.
- **Changed:** nothing.

### the-picnic-mystery: The Picnic Mystery

- **Answers:** Q3 (who took it): never stated; paw prints, bread on his whiskers, and everyone

- **Wrong answers:** Q3's "a bird" fits the crumbs and torn paper, but not the paw prints or

- **Needs the story:** yes.
- **Grade:** a gentle mystery that teaches using clues, which suits inference well.
- **Changed:** the blanket and tree, above.

### the-new-girl: The New Girl

### the-lemonade-stand: The Lemonade Stand

### the-snowy-sidewalk: The Snowy Sidewalk

### the-paper-bridge: The Paper Bridge

### the-rainy-day-show: The Rainy-Day Show

### abuelos-secret: Abuelo's Secret

- **Holds together:** yes. "Abuelo" is explained in the first sentence as Diego's grandfather.
- **Facts checked:** stirring pancake batter too much makes pancakes tough and flat; a few lumps

- **Answers:** Q2 (why they were flat) needs the child to connect Diego's stirring with

### a-house-for-wrens: A House for Wrens

- **Holds together:** yes; build, hang, wait, twigs, then chicks in June.
- **Facts checked:** house wrens are small brown birds with a loud, bubbly song, and they nest
  in boxes, filling them with twigs. The usual entrance hole is 1⅛ inches, a little wider than
  a quarter, which keeps out larger birds.
- **Answers:** Q3 (why the twigs): Mom has already said wrens nest in boxes. Q4 (the peeping):
  chicks, and Lily says "a whole family".
- **Changed:** nothing.

### life-in-a-beehive: Life in a Beehive (nonfiction)

- **Facts checked:** a hive holds thousands of bees (often tens of thousands); a queen can lay
  more than a thousand eggs a day; workers are female, work inside when young and outside
  later; the waggle dance gives direction and distance; nectar is dried by fanning into honey
  and eaten in winter; bees carry pollen that helps plants make fruit. "The largest bee is the
  queen" was not quite right, since drones are bulkier, so it now says "longest".
- **Answers:** Q4 (why save honey): no flowers in winter, and honey comes from flowers. Q5 (no
  bees): fewer apples. Other insects pollinate too, which is why it says "fewer", not "none".
- **Needs the passage:** a child who knows bees could answer Q1 without reading.
- **Changed:** "largest" to "longest".

### the-monarchs-long-trip: The Monarch's Long Trip (nonfiction)

- **Facts checked:** eastern monarchs migrate from Canada and the northern United States to fir
  forests in the mountains of central Mexico, some more than two thousand miles; no single
  butterfly makes the whole trip back north; caterpillars eat only milkweed, which makes
  caterpillars and adults taste bad to birds; milkweed has declined, and planting it helps.
- **Grade:** read at 4.7 at first, too hard. Long sentences were split; it now reads at 3.3.
  The spring trip says the next generations finish it, rather than that the travelers die.
- **Answers:** Q3 (how orange wings help): taste bad plus warning sign. Q4 (why plant milkweed):
  it is the caterpillars' only food.
- **Changed:** the reading level, above.

### the-clever-octopus: The Clever Octopus (nonfiction)

- **Facts checked:** eight arms; suckers that grip and taste; no bones, with the beak the only
  hard part, so it fits through any gap the beak fits through; color and texture change in
  under a second; ink; puzzle-solving and jar-opening in aquariums; veined octopuses carry
  coconut shells to hide in.
- **Grade:** read at 4.2 at first; long sentences were split, and it now reads at 3.7.
- **Answers:** Q3 (why change color): it matches rock or sand and "seems to disappear". Q4 (what
  opening jars shows): it is given as an example of being smart.
- **Changed:** the reading level, above.

## Grade 4

Fifteen passages: eleven stories and four nonfiction passages. Reviewed by Claude, 2026-11-20.
No grade 3 name is used again, and no name is used twice within grade 4.

### the-telescope-in-the-attic: The Telescope in the Attic

- **Holds together:** yes. The missing knob is replaced by the spool, and the spool is what
  Kenji turns to focus. Cloudy all week, clear on Saturday, then the next night.
- **Facts checked:** Galileo saw Jupiter's four largest moons in 1610, more than four hundred
  years ago, and they show as dots in a row in a small telescope. Their positions change from
  night to night.
- **Answers:** Q3 (how he felt): he doesn't want to blink and stays out until called three times.
  Q4 (why the notebook): he draws the moons with the date, twice, and sees that they moved.
- **Wrong answers:** Q2's "specks of dust" is ruled out because Aunt Ruth says they are moons.
- **Needs the story:** a child who knows astronomy could answer Q2 without reading.
- **Changed:** "the brightest light in the sky" became a light Aunt Ruth points out, since the
  Moon or Venus is often brighter than Jupiter.

### pepper-goes-missing: Pepper Goes Missing

- **Holds together:** yes. Sofia cleans the cage, leaves, comes back to it open.
- **Answers:** Q2 (why the door was open) is never stated. Mateo was beside her in the living
  room, and afterward she checks the latch every time and adds a latch rule, so it was her slip.
  Q4 (why shake the bag): guinea pigs squeak at the sound of food.
- **Wrong answers:** Q2's "Mateo opened it" is ruled out because he was with her; "Mom opened it
  to clean" because Sofia had just cleaned it.
- **Grade:** a small worry (losing the class pet) with a happy ending.
- **Changed:** Pepper was first found in a laundry basket. Guinea pigs cannot climb, so she is
  now in a pile of towels on the closet floor. Sofia now sits beside Mateo, to rule out Q2's
  "Mateo opened it".

### the-ball-over-the-fence: The Ball Over the Fence

- **Holds together:** yes. "He never smiled" at the start and "did not see him smile very
  often" at the end fit together: he warms up without changing completely.
- **Answers:** Q2 (why knock): he was about to leave it until he remembered it was his
  grandmother's present. Q4 (what the photo and advice suggest): he fixes the kick at once, and
  the photo shows a young goalkeeper.
- **Wrong answers:** Q4 first had "He does not like having visitors at all", which the Keep Out

- **Grade:** read at 2.9 at first; some short sentences were joined, and it reads at 3.4.
- **Changed:** the reading level and Q4's wrong answer, above.

### the-robot-race: The Robot Race

- **Holds together:** yes. A rubbing left wheel slows that side, so the robot turns left. The

- **Changed:** at 251 words it was just over the minimum; a sentence about the robot's parts was

### the-market-mistake: The Market Mistake

- **Holds together:** the arithmetic works. Peas $3 + beans $4 = $7, change from $10 is $3, she

- **Wrong answers:** Q2's "did not know how to add" is ruled out by "quick at adding"; "did not

- **Grade:** read at 2.9 at first; it now reads at 3.4.

### the-solo: The Solo

### the-kite-that-would-not-fly: The Kite That Would Not Fly

- **Holds together:** yes. The painted dragon's "curling tail" is decoration; the real tail is
  what the kite lacks, and the dad says "a real tail".
- **Facts checked:** a tail adds weight and drag at the bottom, which keeps a kite facing into
  the wind instead of spinning.
- **Answers:** Q3 (why cross his arms): repeated crashes and "ready to go home". Q4 (why the

- **Grade:** read at 2.5 at first; it now reads at 3.3.
- **Changed:** the reading level.

### the-lunchroom-detectives: The Lunchroom Detectives

- **Holds together:** yes. The week of sorting, the chart, the talk, the month-long trial, and
  the tomatoes the following spring (compost takes months).
- **Answers:** Q4 (why stand by the bins): students kept using the trash out of habit. Q5 (what
  helped the tomatoes): compost becomes rich soil that feeds plants.
- **Wrong answers:** Q5's wrong answers (signs, gloves, poster) do not feed plants.
- **Changed:** nothing.

### tiny-tardigrades: Tiny Tardigrades (nonfiction)

- **Facts checked:** eight legs with claws; most under half a millimeter; found from mountains
  to the deep sea, hot springs, Antarctic ice and backyard moss; the dried "tun" state can last
  years; in 2007 (the FOTON-M3 mission) dried tardigrades survived open space, and some revived;
  researchers study them to keep medicines stable when dried.
- **Needs the passage:** Q1 ("water bear") may be known already.
- **Answers:** Q4 (how the tun helps): it lasts until water returns. Q5 (why they might help with
  medicines): they survive drying out.
- **Grade:** read at 5.0 at first; one long sentence was split, and it reads at 4.9.
- **Changed:** the reading level.

### termite-towers: Termite Towers (nonfiction)

- **Facts checked:** mound-building termites in African grasslands (Macrotermes) build mounds
  taller than an adult, with colonies in the millions; workers build with soil and saliva; the
  queen and nest are below the mound; tunnels move air and heat; they farm a fungus on dead
  plant material; the Eastgate Centre in Harare, Zimbabwe, borrowed the idea for cooling.
- **Simplified:** how air moves through a mound is still studied and depends on the day's
  temperature. "Works like a giant chimney" is the usual simple picture.
- **Answers:** Q3 (fungus without airflow): heat would build up, and the fungus needs steady
  warmth. Q4 (why "chimney"): air carries heat out.
- **Changed:** nothing.

### islands-made-of-fire: Islands Made of Fire (nonfiction)

- **Facts checked:** the islands are volcanic and formed over a hot spot; the Pacific Plate moves
  a few inches a year; Kauai is about five million years old; the Big Island is the youngest and
  Kilauea erupts; the next volcano (Kamaʻehuakanaloa, once called Loihi) is about twenty miles
  offshore, its summit more than 3,000 feet down, and expected to surface in tens of thousands
  of years. The nearest continent is more than two thousand miles away.
- **Answers:** Q4 (why Kauai no longer erupts): islands carried away from the hot spot stop
  erupting, and Kauai is the oldest. Q5 (why no one will stand on the new island): tens of
  thousands of years is far longer than a lifetime.
- **Needs the passage:** Q1 (magma) can be known from science class.
- **Grade:** read at 5.9 at first, too hard; long sentences were split, and it reads at 5.0, the
  top of the range. Worth the parent's view on whether it is too hard.
- **Changed:** the reading level, and "thousands of miles from any other land" became "more than
  two thousand miles from the nearest continent", which is accurate.

### emperor-penguin-dads: Emperor Penguin Dads (nonfiction)

- **Facts checked:** the largest penguin; breeds through the Antarctic winter; one egg, passed to
  the male; he holds it on his feet under a brood pouch for about two months without eating and
  can lose close to half his weight over the whole fast; huddles rotate; the female returns
  near hatching and finds him by his call; both parents then feed the chick.
- **Answers:** Q4 (an egg on the ice): it is kept warm on his feet because of the cold, so it
  would chill. Q5 (why the call): thousands of penguins look alike.
- **Wrong answers:** Q5 first had "She cannot see at all in the dark", which a child could argue
  from the winter darkness. It is now "She wants to teach the chick his call".
- **Grade:** read at 5.2 at first; it now reads at 4.7.
- **Changed:** the reading level and Q5's wrong answer, above.

### Grade 4 as a whole

- **Balance:** 15 passages, each with 5 questions: 30 stated, 30 inferred, and 15 others
  (7 vocabulary, 4 sequence, 4 main idea). Four passages are nonfiction (tardigrades, termite
  mounds, Hawaiian volcanoes, emperor penguins).
- **Settings and people:** an attic and backyard, a classroom pet, neighbors, a school robot race,
  a farmers market, a concert, a bookstore and museum, a bake sale, chess at home, a park hill,
  a school cafeteria.
- **Length and level:** 270 to 315 words each, reading between grade 3.3 and 5.0.
- **Themes:** honesty (the market, the ball), handling mistakes and nerves (the solo, the robot),
  not judging people early (the ball, chess), problem solving (the kite, the bake sale, the
  lunchroom).
- **For the parent's review:** the islands passage (5.0) is the hardest. The solo (3.3) and the
  kite (3.3) are the easiest. Three questions can be answered from general knowledge: the
  telescope's Q2 (Jupiter's moons), the islands' Q1 (magma) and the tardigrades' Q1 (water bear).
