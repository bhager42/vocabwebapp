# Reading story reviews

Every story gets a review before it ships (PLAN.md Phase 12). `npm run reading:check` covers the
mechanical rules; this file records the checks a script cannot make:

1. **Holds together:** names, places, the order of events and facts agree throughout, and there
   is a clear beginning, middle and end.
2. **Answers are right:** each literal answer is in the story, and each inference answer is the
   one best answer the story supports, without being written out in it.
3. **No second right answer:** each wrong answer was checked against the story on its own.
4. **Needs the story:** no question can be answered from general knowledge alone.
5. **Suits the grade:** words, sentence length and themes; nothing frightening or mature.
6. **Original:** not a retelling of a known book, film or game.

Each entry lists anything that was changed. The parent's review comes after this one.

## Grade 3

The first five stories were the pilot; the other ten complete the grade. Fifteen in all:
eleven stories and four nonfiction passages. Reviewed by Claude, 2026-11-16.

### the-roof-garden: The Roof Garden

- **Holds together:** yes. The pots go up to the roof, and Grandpa is downstairs when Priya
  cheers and when she runs down to tell him. Three of the six pots are emptied and replanted.
  Mrs. Lee lives on the same floor, so she is one of the neighbors given tomatoes.
- **Answers:** Q2 (why she cheered) follows straight after the sprouts appear. Q3 (what happened
  to the sprouts) is never stated; torn leaves, the pigeon sitting there and Grandpa's net all
  point to the pigeon.
- **Wrong answers:** Q2's "school was over for the year" does not fit: it is spring and she
  still goes to school. Q3's "wind" would not pull sprouts out and leave torn leaves, and nothing
  suggests Priya forgot to water them, since she checked every day.
- **Needs the story:** yes; seeds, the pigeon and the net are all specific to it.
- **Grade:** plain words, family setting, a small problem solved.
- **Changed:** nothing.

### the-missing-library-book: The Missing Library Book

- **Holds together:** fixed one slip. Leo was outside on the porch, then "just then" heard a
  voice from his sister's room. He now goes back inside first.
- **Answers:** Q3 (why he worried): he has looked everywhere and wonders what to tell the
  librarian. Q4 (why Nina's face fell): it falls the moment he asks for the book back and lifts
  when he offers her a library trip.
- **Wrong answers:** Q3's "had not finished reading it" is ruled out because he read it three
  times. Q4's "scared of the sharks" is ruled out because she is reassuring her teddy bear.
- **Needs the story:** yes.
- **Grade:** read at 1.3 at first, below the grade 3 range. Some short sentences were joined
  and a few richer words added ("photographs", "cushions"); it now reads at 2.0, the bottom of
  the range. Worth the parent's view on whether it is still too easy.
- **Changed:** the porch slip above. The sequence question was rewritten too ("Which of these
  did Leo do first?"), because the old one asked what happened "right after" the porch, and
  that answer changed with the fix.

### busy-builders: Busy Builders (nonfiction)

- **Holds together:** yes; body, then dams, then lodges, then how beavers help other animals.
- **Facts checked:** beavers live across much of North America; their front teeth never stop
  growing; their back feet are webbed; dams are built from sticks, mud and stones; lodge
  entrances are under water; drained beaver ponds leave rich soil where meadows grow.
  "In the middle of the pond" was too absolute, since lodges are also built at the edge of a
  pond, so it now says "Out in the pond, beavers often build…".
- **Answers:** Q4 (why the doors are under water) is close to stated ("most other animals
  cannot"), but the reason, safety, still has to be worked out. Q5 (if the dam broke) needs the
  child to see that the pond exists only because the dam holds the water back.
- **Wrong answers:** none fit the text. Q4's "beavers are not able to climb" is not in the
  passage and does not explain an underwater door.
- **Needs the story:** mostly. A child who already knows about beavers could answer Q1 and Q4
  without reading. Nonfiction always carries some of that; flagging it for the parent.
- **Grade:** "lodge" is taught in the passage and asked as the vocabulary question.
- **Changed:** "In the middle of the pond", above.

### the-fun-run: The Fun Run

- **Holds together:** yes; two laps, she leads after the first and falls in the second.
- **Answers:** Q3 (why Ava stopped): she asks if Rosa is okay and holds out her hand. Q4 (why
  the double knot): her lace came loose and tripped her.
- **Wrong answers:** Q1's "slipped in a puddle" does not fit a bright, warm day. Q3's "hurt her
  own knee" does not fit, since it is Rosa's knee that stings.
- **Needs the story:** Q5 (the lesson) could be guessed by a child who has heard many stories
  like this. That is acceptable for a main-idea question, whose wrong answers are clearly off.
- **Grade:** a fall and tears, but nothing more upsetting; the ending is warm.
- **Changed:** nothing.

### the-picnic-mystery: The Picnic Mystery

- **Holds together:** fixed one point. The blanket was under the tree, yet the paw prints led
  from the blanket toward that same tree. The blanket is now on the grass near a big oak, and
  Biscuit rests in its shade.
- **Answers:** Q3 (who took it): never stated; paw prints, bread on his whiskers, and everyone
  else at the playground. Q4 (why he would not look): lying still and looking away after eating
  the sandwich.
- **Wrong answers:** Q3's "a bird" fits the crumbs and torn paper, but not the paw prints or
  the bread on Biscuit's whiskers. The question says "most likely" for this reason. Q4's
  options do not fit the story.
- **Needs the story:** yes.
- **Grade:** a gentle mystery that teaches using clues, which suits inference well.
- **Changed:** the blanket and tree, above.

### the-new-girl: The New Girl

- **Holds together:** yes; first day, the rest of the week, then Friday.
- **Answers:** Q2 (how she felt) is never stated; her quiet voice, looking at her shoes and
  sitting alone point to shy and lonely. Q4 (why she will like it here) follows from the week
  spent with Marcus.
- **Wrong answers:** Q2's "proud and excited" does not fit any of those clues. Q4's "her desk
  was by a window" is true in the story but is never given as a reason. Q5's "a cat who
  rescues lost kittens" is the comic, not the story, and makes a fair trap.
- **Grade:** a familiar school situation with a warm ending.
- **Changed:** nothing.

### the-lemonade-stand: The Lemonade Stand

- **Holds together:** yes. Eleven dollars at 50 cents is 22 cups; that fits one sale on their
  street plus a soccer team and parents, though two jugs of lemonade would need to be large.
- **Answers:** Q4 (why it sold at the park): a hot day and red-faced players. Q5 (what Noah
  learned): the quiet street against the busy park, in his own words at the end.
- **Wrong answers:** none fit. Q2 asks about "her smile was melting", a figure of speech, which
  counts as a vocabulary question.
- **Changed:** nothing.

### the-snowy-sidewalk: The Snowy Sidewalk

- **Holds together:** yes. The daughter takes Mr. Garcia to lunch, and later the same daughter
  brings the cocoa.
- **Answers:** Q2 (why he looked at the snow): his cane, the deep snow and the long path to the
  car. Q3 (why she looked from her sled to him): she chooses to help first and sled later.
- **Wrong answers:** none fit; "waiting for the mail" has no support.
- **Grade:** read at 1.3 at first, too easy. Some short sentences were joined; it now reads at
  2.1.
- **Changed:** the reading level, above. The girl was renamed from Ava to Maya, because The Fun
  Run already has an Ava.

### the-paper-bridge: The Paper Bridge

- **Holds together:** yes.
- **Facts checked:** folding paper into pleats does make it far stiffer, and corrugated
  roofing and folded-plate structures use the same idea, so Ms. Patel's line is fair.
- **Answers:** Q2 (why the first bridge failed): flat paper sagged under one penny. Q4 (why Jada
  folds a new sheet): her bridge failed the same way, and she has just seen the folded one work.
- **Wrong answers:** Q4's "a fan for her sister" is a deliberate trap: the sister who makes fans
  is Tomás's.
- **Note:** in Q1 the right answer is the longest choice by a word or two. It is within the rule,
  but worth watching.
- **Changed:** nothing.

### the-rainy-day-show: The Rainy-Day Show

- **Holds together:** yes; the zoo is off, the show is at three, then the rain stops.
- **Answers:** Q4 (bored, then happy): his groan at the start and "maybe tomorrow" at the end.
  Q5 (why "maybe tomorrow"): he wants to keep playing.
- **Wrong answers:** Q5's "had never liked the zoo" is ruled out because he had planned to go.
- **Changed:** nothing.

### abuelos-secret: Abuelo's Secret

- **Holds together:** yes. "Abuelo" is explained in the first sentence as Diego's grandfather.
- **Facts checked:** stirring pancake batter too much makes pancakes tough and flat; a few lumps
  are normal advice.
- **Answers:** Q2 (why they were flat) needs the child to connect Diego's stirring with
  Abuelo's rule. Q4 (what Abuelo thought): the thumbs-up.
- **Wrong answers:** Q2's "the pan was not hot enough" is ruled out because the pancakes
  sizzled. The explanation says so.
- **Changed:** nothing.

### a-house-for-wrens: A House for Wrens

- **Holds together:** yes; build, hang, wait, twigs, then chicks in June.
- **Facts checked:** house wrens are small brown birds with a loud, bubbly song, and they nest
  in boxes, filling them with twigs. The usual entrance hole is 1⅛ inches, a little wider than
  a quarter, which keeps out larger birds.
- **Answers:** Q3 (why the twigs): Mom has already said wrens nest in boxes. Q4 (the peeping):
  chicks, and Lily says "a whole family".
- **Changed:** nothing.

### life-in-a-beehive: Life in a Beehive (nonfiction)

- **Facts checked:** a hive holds thousands of bees (often tens of thousands); a queen can lay
  more than a thousand eggs a day; workers are female, work inside when young and outside
  later; the waggle dance gives direction and distance; nectar is dried by fanning into honey
  and eaten in winter; bees carry pollen that helps plants make fruit. "The largest bee is the
  queen" was not quite right, since drones are bulkier, so it now says "longest".
- **Answers:** Q4 (why save honey): no flowers in winter, and honey comes from flowers. Q5 (no
  bees): fewer apples. Other insects pollinate too, which is why it says "fewer", not "none".
- **Needs the passage:** a child who knows bees could answer Q1 without reading.
- **Changed:** "largest" to "longest".

### the-monarchs-long-trip: The Monarch's Long Trip (nonfiction)

- **Facts checked:** eastern monarchs migrate from Canada and the northern United States to fir
  forests in the mountains of central Mexico, some more than two thousand miles; no single
  butterfly makes the whole trip back north; caterpillars eat only milkweed, which makes
  caterpillars and adults taste bad to birds; milkweed has declined, and planting it helps.
- **Grade:** read at 4.7 at first, too hard. Long sentences were split; it now reads at 3.3.
  The spring trip says the next generations finish it, rather than that the travelers die.
- **Answers:** Q3 (how orange wings help): taste bad plus warning sign. Q4 (why plant milkweed):
  it is the caterpillars' only food.
- **Changed:** the reading level, above.

### the-clever-octopus: The Clever Octopus (nonfiction)

- **Facts checked:** eight arms; suckers that grip and taste; no bones, with the beak the only
  hard part, so it fits through any gap the beak fits through; color and texture change in
  under a second; ink; puzzle-solving and jar-opening in aquariums; veined octopuses carry
  coconut shells to hide in.
- **Grade:** read at 4.2 at first; long sentences were split, and it now reads at 3.7.
- **Answers:** Q3 (why change color): it matches rock or sand and "seems to disappear". Q4 (what
  opening jars shows): it is given as an example of being smart.
- **Changed:** the reading level, above.

### Grade 3 as a whole

- **Balance:** 15 passages, each with 5 questions: 30 stated, 30 inferred, and 15 others
  (6 vocabulary, 6 sequence, 3 main idea). Four passages are nonfiction (beavers, honeybees,
  monarchs, octopuses).
- **Settings and people:** city apartment, suburbs, school, park, snow day, kitchen, garden,
  plus nature. Children's names and families are varied, and no name is used twice.
- **Reading level:** every passage reads between grade 2.1 and 3.7.
- **For the parent's review:** the library book (2.3) and the snowy sidewalk (2.1) are the
  easiest. The four nonfiction passages are where a child who already knows the topic can
  answer a question or two without reading.
